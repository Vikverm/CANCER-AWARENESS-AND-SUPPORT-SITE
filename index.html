Common Cancer Types

Know the Most Common Types of Cancer

Learning about different types of cancer helps people recognize symptoms early and seek timely medical advice.

Breast Cancer

One of the most common cancers affecting women. Early screening improves treatment outcomes.

Lung Cancer

Smoking remains one of the major risk factors. Quitting tobacco greatly reduces the risk.

Blood Cancer

Includes leukemia, lymphoma and myeloma. Early diagnosis is essential.

Brain Cancer

Affects the brain and nervous system. Symptoms vary depending on the tumor location.

Skin Cancer

Protect your skin from excessive UV exposure and perform regular skin checks.

Stomach Cancer

Healthy eating habits and medical checkups help reduce risk.

Colon Cancer

Regular screening is important, especially for older adults.

Prostate Cancer

Common in older men and often detected through routine screening.

Frequently Asked Questions

Cancer Awareness Knowledge Center

Find answers to the most commonly asked questions about cancer awareness, prevention, diagnosis, screening and treatment.

What is cancer?

Cancer is a group of diseases where abnormal cells grow uncontrollably and may spread to other parts of the body.

Can cancer be prevented?

Many cancers can be prevented by avoiding tobacco, maintaining a healthy diet, exercising regularly, limiting alcohol and participating in recommended screening programs.

What are common warning signs?

Persistent cough, unexplained weight loss, unusual bleeding, long-lasting fatigue, changes in moles, lumps and persistent pain should be evaluated by a healthcare professional.

Is early detection important?

Yes. Early detection often improves treatment options and health outcomes.

Is smoking the biggest risk factor?

Tobacco use is one of the leading preventable causes of cancer and increases the risk of several cancer types.

Does family history increase cancer risk?

Some cancers have hereditary risk factors. Discuss your family history with a healthcare provider to determine whether additional screening may be appropriate.

Can young people get cancer?

Yes. Although cancer becomes more common with age, children, teenagers and young adults can also develop certain types of cancer.

Does eating healthy eliminate cancer risk?

No. A healthy diet may lower the risk of some cancers, but it cannot completely eliminate the possibility of developing cancer.

Are all tumors cancerous?

No. Tumors can be benign (non-cancerous) or malignant (cancerous). A medical evaluation is required to determine the difference.

Can exercise reduce cancer risk?

Regular physical activity supports overall health and is associated with a lower risk of several types of cancer.
